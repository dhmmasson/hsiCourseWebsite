Concept of Operations (CONOPS)

Class on developing a Concept of Operations (CONOPS).
Author

D. Masson

Concept of Operations (CONOPS)

Synopsis

This session introduces the Concept of Operations (ConOps) as a central yet evolving artifact in systems engineering. Students explore ConOps primarily from the user’s perspective: who uses it, for what purposes, and how its form and content change across the system life-cycle. Rather than treating ConOps as a fixed document, the class frames it as a mutable boundary object that mediates understanding between stakeholders, decision-makers, engineers, and operators. Emphasis is placed on narrative intent, operational meaning, and context, rather than on prescriptive templates.

Session Objectives

By the end of the session, students should be able to: - Explain what a ConOps is and why it exists, beyond formal definitions. - Identify different ConOps/OpsCon users and articulate their needs (decisions, actions, coordination). - Situate a ConOps along the life-cycle continuum (early concept, acquisition, operations, evolution, retirement). - Compare multiple ConOps artifacts and recognize how perspective, maturity, and purpose shape them. - Critically assess whether a given ConOps format effectively serves its intended users.

Key Concepts

  • Concept of Operations vs. Operational Concept (ConOps / OpsCon)
  • User-centered viewpoint and stakeholder diversity
  • Narrative vs. formalized representations
  • Life-cycle evolution of ConOps
  • ConOps as a communication and alignment artifact
  • Relationships between ConOps, requirements, and architecture
  • Standards and guidance (e.g. IEEE, ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288, AIAA)
  • Consistency, congruence, and traceability without over-specification

In-Class Activity Plan – Concept of Operations (ConOps)

1. Opening Exposure – Industrial Perspective (≈20 min)

Activity - Watch a ~20-minute video presenting how ConOps is used in an industrial / defense context. - Video link: Case Study Video Quick debrief (5 min) - First reactions: What surprised you? - What seemed implicit rather than explicitly stated? - Where does ConOps appear in the life cycle? - What other artifacts are mentioned alongside ConOps?

2. Concept Exploration – Definitions & Conceptual Landscape (≈35–45 min)

Objective

Students independently explore how ConOps is defined, framed, and related to other concepts across domains and standards.

Activity

  • Teams perform web-based exploration (standards, academic sources, industry blogs, military doctrine, systems engineering communities).
  • Each team builds a mind map on MIRO board:
    • Core concept: Concept of Operations (ConOps)
    • Related concepts
    • Source-backed definitions (each node must reference its source)

MIRO Board – Guiding Questions (to seed exploration)

Write these questions directly on the MIRO board as prompts:

Core Definition

  • How is ConOps defined?
  • Who defines it (standard, military, industry, academia)? Add the source (url, document).
  • Is ConOps defined as a document, a description, a model, or a process?

Related Concepts

  • What is the relationship between:
    • ConOps and OpsCon?
    • ConOps and requirements?
    • ConOps and architecture?
    • ConOps and scenarios / use cases?
  • Where do boundaries become unclear or overlap?

Perspective & Intent

  • From whose point of view is ConOps written?
  • Who is not the intended reader?
  • What assumptions about the system or its context are embedded?

Life-Cycle Positioning

  • At which stages of the life cycle is ConOps mentioned or emphasized?
  • Does its role change between:
    • Early concept / R&D
    • Acquisition
    • Operations
    • Evolution / retirement?

Form & Representation

  • In what forms does ConOps appear?
    • Narrative text
    • Graphics / diagrams
    • Models (e.g., SysML)
  • What seems to drive the choice of form?

Short Wrap-Up & Synthesis (10 min)

  • Each team briefly presents one unexpected insight from their map.
  • Instructor synthesizes:
    • Commonalities
    • Tensions
    • Contradictions across definitions

3. Analysis of Existing ConOps & Research Papers (≈45–60 min)

Objective

Move from definition to usage, focusing on ConOps as a mutable artefact shaped by users, needs, and life-cycle context.

Activity

  • Teams analyze provided research papers and real ConOps examples.
  • Each team applies a shared analytical framework.

Analysis Framework (Questions to Answer)

USERS

  • Who are the explicit users of this ConOps?
  • Who are the implicit users?
  • Are there multiple user perspectives coexisting?
  • How do user needs differ across the life cycle?

PERSPECTIVES

  • Operational
  • Managerial / decision-making
  • Engineering / design
  • Human-System Integration (HSI)
  • Regulatory / certification

LIFE CYCLE

  • At what life-cycle stage does this ConOps appear?
  • Does it anticipate future stages?
  • Is it stable or clearly evolving?

NEEDS

  • What decisions does this ConOps support?
  • What actions does it enable or constrain?
  • Are HSI concerns explicit or implicit?
  • Which human roles are described, ignored, or abstracted away?

PRODUCER / CONSUMER VIEW

  • Who produced the ConOps?
  • Who consumes it?
  • Is there a mismatch between producer intent and consumer use?

CONOPS AS AN ARTEFACT

  • Level of abstraction (high-level vision vs. operational detail)
  • Degree of formalism
  • Is it:
    • Descriptive?
    • Prescriptive?
    • Exploratory?

FORM & REPRESENTATION

  • Narrative text
  • Graphics
  • Models
  • Hybrid
  • What does the chosen form emphasize or hide?

WHY / HOW / WHAT

  • Why does this ConOps exist?
  • How is it meant to be used?
  • What does it explicitly define—and what does it leave open?

CONTINUUM

  • Where does this ConOps sit on a continuum between:
    • Vision ↔︎ Execution
    • Human-centered ↔︎ System-centered
    • Informal ↔︎ Formal

4. Wrap-Up & Collective Reflection (≈15 min)

Class discussion - What patterns emerge across different ConOps? - In what ways does ConOps “mutate” over time? - When does ConOps stop being useful—or become dangerous?

Final Integrative Question (HSI Focus)

From your perspective: > What should be explicitly present in a ConOps to properly address Human-System Integration (HSI)?

Prompt dimensions: - Human roles and responsibilities - Cognitive workload and decision authority - Coordination and communication - Evolution of human involvement across the life cycle

Students answer briefly in teams, then share highlights.

Notes

  • There is no single “correct” ConOps format; effectiveness depends on context and audience.
  • Early-stage ConOps tend to be exploratory and narrative; later ones often stabilize but may lose accessibility.
  • Treat ConOps as a living artifact that supports reasoning, communication, and learning—not merely compliance.
  • Tension between formalization (for rigor and simulation) and storytelling (for shared understanding) is intentional and productive.
